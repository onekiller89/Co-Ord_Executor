### Summary
Sandeep Swadia shares 20 "cheat codes" distilled from his journey from homelessness in Mumbai to MIT graduate and multi-millionaire tech executive. The advice spans personal finance, career strategy, mindset, relationships, and happiness philosophy. The core thread is that long-term compounding — in money, skills, relationships, and mindset — consistently outperforms short-term optimisation.

### Key Insights

- **Investing beats saving**: $5,000/year in an S&P 500 index fund from your 20s compounds to ~$1.4M by retirement. The only rules: buy index funds, stay in the market, and never panic-sell.
- **Passion follows mastery, not the other way around**: Only 4% of people can monetise their existing passion. Build skills first — passion and fulfilment follow excellence.
- **Equity > Salary**: Salary buys lifestyle; equity builds wealth. Even small ownership stakes shift your mindset from employee to owner, which changes how you work.
- **Career = Investment Portfolio**: Treat early-career years as paid learning. Skills and connections compound just like money — 15,000 hours invested early paid off decades later.
- **Geography matters**: Physical proximity to opportunity hubs accelerates career growth in ways remote work cannot replicate, especially for ambitious early-career professionals.
- **The FBI Framework for money**: Categorise spending into four buckets — Emergency, Essentials, Equity, Enjoyment — and only fund enjoyment after the first three are covered.
- **The Happiness Equation**: H = O/D (Happiness = What You Own ÷ What You Desire). Managing desires is more achievable than endlessly growing ownership. When desires approach zero, happiness approaches infinity.
- **Partner choice is the highest-leverage life decision**: Choosing the wrong life partner creates emotional bankruptcy. Use slow, rational System 2 thinking — not fast, emotional System 1 — for this decision.

- [ ] Automate savings so contributions are deducted *before* your paycheck hits your spending account

- [ ] Cancel or cut any Enjoyment spending that is funded before Emergency and Equity buckets are filled
- [ ] Identify one high-value, monetisable skill and commit to deliberate daily practice (even 30–60 minutes/day)
