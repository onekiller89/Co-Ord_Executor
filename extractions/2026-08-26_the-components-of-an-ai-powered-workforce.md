# The Components of an AI Powered Workforce

### Summary
This presentation from an AI:ROI conference outlines a strategic framework for building an AI-powered workforce, directed at Heads of AI and C-suite executives. The core argument is that AI transformation fails not because of technology gaps but because of capability, confidence, and change management gaps. The speakers introduce the concept of the "super company" and a practical three-horizon playbook (30/90/365 days) for driving real, sustained AI adoption across an organisation.

---

### Key Insights

- **Lower short-term, raise medium-term expectations**: The near-term (3–9 months) is overhyped, but most CEOs dramatically underestimate AI's medium-to-long-term value if transformation is managed well.
- **Transformation is 12 one-month programs, not one annual plan**: Consistent, repeated monthly effort beats a single hackathon or annual initiative — you can't skip steps.
- **The perception gap is real and dangerous**: C-suite leaders consistently believe AI adoption is further along than managers and employees actually report. This misalignment undermines the Head of AI role.
- **Usage ≠ value**: Most employees use AI as a search engine replacement or summarisation tool. Depth and sophistication of use is the real challenge, not access or awareness.
- **The Head of AI sits at the hardest intersection**: Strategy, technology, and human behaviour change — with accountability for outcomes across teams they don't directly manage.
- **Managers are the multiplier or the bottleneck**: If managers aren't using AI themselves and normalising it on their teams, transformation stalls at the initiative level.
- **Cut first, then create**: Eliminate unnecessary workflows, steps, and vendors before pursuing growth use cases. Move through the efficiency phase fast to unlock the real value of creation and growth.
- **Every knowledge worker needs an AI Chief of Staff**: Building personal AI agents connected to relevant data sources is the highest-leverage individual unlock and a gateway to appreciating AI's full capability.
- **The transformation pyramid has dependencies**: Augment workforce → Automate workflows → Reinvent core business processes. Skipping the foundation means higher-order transformation won't stick.

### Actions

- [ ] Audit your current AI adoption honestly — survey managers AND frontline employees separately and compare results to leadership perception
- [ ] Establish a clear organisational "why for AI" with measurable success metrics before deploying further tooling
- [ ] Ensure every knowledge worker has access to a best-in-class AI tool (e.g., GPT Enterprise, Microsoft Copilot Pro, Claude Teams)
- [ ] Build or assign someone to build a personal AI Chief of Staff for each C-suite member, connected to their key data sources
- [ ] Hire or formally designate a Head of AI with budget, headcount support, and cross-functional mandate
- [ ] Train managers first — give them a rubric for holding teams accountable for AI use before rolling out broad employee training
- [ ] Run a company-wide hackathon (even 1–5 days) to spike adoption, then follow up with monthly momentum activities
- [ ] Convert your annual AI plan into 12 monthly transformation plans with specific, tracked deliverables each month
- [ ] Identify your "super users" (top proficient AI users) and create visible channels for them to share what's working
- [ ] Prepare written answers to the hard employee questions (job security, environmental cost, role change) before your next all-hands meeting
- [ ] Map your organisation's position on the maturity curve (AI-assisted search → workflow building → agent management → work delegation)
- [ ] Prioritise efficiency/workflow cuts over growth use cases initially — identify 3–5 workflows where AI can reduce non-core time (e.g., pre-sales prep)

### Implementation Prompts

#### Prompt 1: AI Adoption Perception Gap Audit
*Surfaces the dangerous disconnect between leadership perception and ground-level reality so you can diagnose where your transformation actually stands before investing further.*

> Create a structured internal survey framework to identify the AI adoption perception gap in my organisation. Produce two separate survey versions: one for C-suite/senior leaders and one for managers and individual contributors. Each should have 8–10 questions covering: current AI tool usage frequency, perceived company-wide adoption level, confidence in using AI for role-specific tasks, clarity on AI policies and acceptable use, and awareness of the company's AI strategy and goals. Output the questions in a table format comparing what each group is being asked, and include a scoring rubric to quantify the gap between leadership perception and employee reality. Also suggest 3 follow-up actions based on possible gap sizes (small/medium/large).

---

#### Prompt 2: 12-Month AI Transformation Monthly Plan
*Converts the abstract idea of "12 one-month programs" into a concrete, actionable monthly cadence that a Head of AI can actually execute and report on.*

> Build a detailed 12-month AI transformation calendar for a mid-sized company (500–2,000 employees) structured as 12 separate one-month programs. For each month, include: the primary theme or focus area, 3–5 concrete actions to complete that month, the key stakeholder group targeted (leadership/managers/all employees/IT), a measurable milestone or success indicator, and a common failure mode to watch for. Structure it across three horizons: Months 1 (foundation setting), Months 2–3 (activation and baselining), and Months 4–12 (sustained adoption, workflow automation, and agent deployment). Output as a structured table. Avoid vague advice — every action should be specific enough to assign to a person with a deadline.

---

#### Prompt 3: AI Chief of Staff Agent Blueprint
*Helps every executive or knowledge worker actually build the "AI Chief of Staff" concept described in the talk — the highest-leverage individual productivity unlock.*

> Design a step-by-step implementation blueprint for building a personal AI Chief of Staff agent for a senior business executive. Include: (1) the ideal data sources to connect (calendar, email, CRM, meeting notes, project trackers, industry news feeds), (2) the core use cases it should handle (meeting prep, email triage, weekly briefings, decision support, draft communications), (3) recommended tooling options for 2025 (e.g., ChatGPT with memory + connectors, Claude Projects, Microsoft Copilot, custom GPT), (4) a sample system prompt the executive would use to configure their AI Chief of Staff persona, and (5) a suggested daily workflow showing when and how the executive interacts with their AI Chief of Staff throughout the day. Make the system prompt copy-paste ready and role-specific for a CEO or Head of AI.

---

#### Prompt 4: Manager Activation Playbook
*Managers are identified as the biggest multiplier or bottleneck — this prompt builds the concrete enablement programme to get them using AI first and reinforcing it with their teams.*

> Create a practical 30-day Manager AI Activation Playbook designed to be run before a broader employee rollout. The playbook should include: Week 1 — a half-day manager workshop agenda with hands-on AI exercises relevant to their management tasks (1:1 prep, performance feedback drafting, team status summaries, hiring interview prep). Week 2 — a set of 5 daily "AI habits" managers should practise and log. Week 3 — a rubric managers can use to set expectations with their direct reports about AI use in their roles. Week 4 — a team challenge managers run with their own teams to create early momentum. Also include: a list of 5 questions managers should be able to answer confidently about AI before the broader rollout, and a simple accountability check-in template they use in weekly team meetings to normalise AI use.

---

#### Prompt 5: Workforce AI Maturity Assessment Tool
*Helps a Head of AI quickly place their organisation on the maturity curve — from AI-assisted search through to full agent-managed workflows — to prioritise interventions correctly.*

> Build a Workforce AI Maturity Assessment tool for internal use by a Head of AI. It should assess an organisation across 6 maturity levels: (1) AI-assisted search and basic prompting, (2) Regular task augmentation, (3) Workflow integration and prompt sophistication, (4) Workflow building and automation, (5) Agent management and delegation, (6) Agents doing real work with humans in the loop. For each level, define: observable employee behaviours that indicate that level, example tools and use cases typical at that level, the key bottleneck preventing progression to the next level, and 2–3 specific interventions to move to the next level. Output as a one-page diagnostic framework a Head of AI could use in a 30-minute leadership review. Include a simple scoring method (e.g., % of workforce at each level) to generate an overall maturity score.

---

#### Prompt 6: CEO/Leadership "Tough Questions" Answer Framework
*Prepares leaders with credible, honest answers to the employee questions about job security and AI impact that are silently blocking adoption across the workforce.*

> Draft a leadership communication framework to help CEOs and senior leaders confidently answer the hardest employee questions about AI transformation. Cover these specific questions: (1) Will AI take my job? (2) What does this mean for my career growth here? (3) What happens to people whose roles change significantly? (4) What are the environmental costs of AI and does the company care? (5) How do I know if I'm using AI in a way that's safe and compliant? For each question, provide: a recommended honest and direct answer framed from a leadership perspective, the psychological need the employee is expressing underneath the question, what NOT to say (responses that erode trust), and a follow-up action the leader should take after giving the answer. Format as a prep sheet a CEO could review for 15 minutes before an all-hands meeting.

---

#### Prompt 7: "Cut First" Workflow Efficiency Audit
*Operationalises the "cut first, then create" principle by identifying specific workflows where AI can eliminate low-value work and free up time for higher-value creation.*

> Design a structured Workflow Efficiency Audit framework that a Head of AI or department leader can run with any team to identify where AI can eliminate low-value tasks before pursuing growth use cases. The audit should: (1) Map how employees in a given role actually spend their time across a typical week (provide a time-tracking template broken into creation, communication, coordination, analysis, and administration categories), (2) Identify which tasks are repetitive, rules-based, or information-retrieval focused — prime candidates for AI automation or augmentation, (3) Score each task on: time consumed per week, AI replaceability (high/medium/low), strategic value (high/medium/low), and emotional/relationship sensitivity, (4) Output a prioritised list of "cut candidates" — tasks to automate or eliminate — versus "create opportunities" — time freed up to reinvest in higher-value work. Include a worked example for a sales team showing before/after time allocation. Make the template copy-paste ready for use in a team workshop.

---

#### Prompt 8: Super Company Metrics Dashboard Design
*Translates the abstract "super company" vision into a concrete measurement framework so the Head of AI can track leading indicators and report progress to leadership.*

> Design an AI Transformation Metrics Dashboard for a Head of AI to use in monthly reporting to the CEO and board. The dashboard should track leading indicators across four categories: (1) Adoption — weekly active AI users as % of workforce, tool activation rates by department, (2) Proficiency — % of employees at each maturity level, champion/power user identification, (3) Agent Activity — number of automated workflows live, volume of tasks handled by agents vs. humans, (4) Business Impact — time saved per role per week, output quality indicators, and where possible, revenue or cost impact attributed to AI. For each metric, include: how to measure it (data source and method), reporting frequency, what "good" looks like at 90 days vs. 12 months, and a red/amber/green threshold guide. Also suggest a one-page executive summary template that translates these metrics into a narrative a CEO can share with a board. Keep the dashboard practical — avoid metrics that require months of instrumentation to collect.
