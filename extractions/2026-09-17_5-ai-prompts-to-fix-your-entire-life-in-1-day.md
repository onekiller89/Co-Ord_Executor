### Summary
This video presents five AI-powered prompts designed to tackle deep personal blind spots — self-deception, relationship conflict, identity reinvention, difficult decisions, and processing failure. Each prompt turns an AI assistant into a structured thinking partner (investigator, counterparty, mapmaker, simulator, or debrief partner) that asks probing questions rather than offering flattery. The core philosophy is that AI can help surface what your own biased mind hides from you, but human judgment and real-world action must follow.

---

### Key Insights

- **AI as a neutral third party, not an oracle** — Your mind protects its own stories; AI can simulate a detached investigator's perspective. Treat its output as a hypothesis to test, not a diagnosis to accept.
- **Self-deception fades under concrete negative feedback** — Research from *Frontiers in Psychology* shows real, specific critical feedback (not social media noise) measurably reduces self-deception. Keeping a personal "hard feedback log" is a practical tool.
- **Every conflict has four layers** — What you meant, what you said, what they heard, and what they interpreted. AI can argue the other person's case, exposing the weakest part of your own argument before a real conversation.
- **The "end of history illusion" keeps you stuck** — Most people assume they've reached their final identity. History shows otherwise. Treating roles as "clothes, not skin" allows for repeated reinvention without existential crisis.
- **Pre-mortem over post-mortem** — Before making a major decision, simulate failure first. Gary Klein's pre-mortem technique (imagining the decision failed 2 years later and working backward) reveals risks you're too excited or afraid to see.
- **One-way vs. two-way doors** — Jeff Bezos's framework: move fast on reversible decisions, slow down only on truly irreversible ones. Most decisions are two-way doors.
- **Writing about pain has measurable health benefits** — UT Austin research shows that writing about a painful experience for 15 minutes over 4 days produces significantly better health outcomes months later. AI can be the structured writing partner for this.
- **Pain ≠ suffering** — Pain is the event; suffering is the story you keep retelling yourself. The goal is to carry forward lessons without letting wounds define your identity.

### Implementation Prompts

#### Prompt 1: Behavioral Pattern Investigator
*Surfaces the hidden "why" behind a recurring self-defeating behaviour using a structured, evidence-based interview — because your own rationalisation will protect the pattern if unchallenged.*

> Act as an investigator for my behavior. This is the pattern I keep repeating: [INSERT YOUR PATTERN — e.g., "I avoid giving candid feedback to team members even when I know it would help them" or "I can't maintain a consistent workout routine"]. I want to understand three things: (1) where my blind spots may be, (2) why I keep avoiding them, and (3) how I can catch them before they're triggered. Interview me one question at a time. Unpack my answer and based on it, ask me the next better question. Use only my answers as evidence — do not assume or invent details about me. When you have enough information, give me your best hypotheses, the evidence behind each one, and the evidence that is still missing. Be respectful, candid, and direct. Do not motivate me or flatter me. Do not offer solutions until I ask for them.

---

#### Prompt 2: Relationship Conflict Counterparty Simulator
*Forces you to genuinely stress-test your side of a conflict by having AI argue the other person's case rigorously — based on Eli Finkel's research that taking a neutral third-party view stops relationship decline.*

> Act as the person I am in conflict with (or trying to communicate with). Do not pretend you know their inner life — work only from what I tell you. Here is the situation in my own words: [DESCRIBE THE CONFLICT OR CONVERSATION]. Here is what I plan to say: [YOUR INTENDED MESSAGE OR ARGUMENT]. Do not take my side. Argue their case as they might — assuming they are smart, fair, and have reasons that make sense to them. Then tell me: (1) what they most likely feel that I am not seeing, (2) the weakest part of my argument, and (3) what they may need to hear from me to feel understood. Do not flatter me. Do not turn this into a therapy session. Help me rehearse the actual conversation so I am prepared for real pushback.

---

#### Prompt 3: Identity Transformation Mapmaker
*Creates a practical bridge between your current identity and a new chapter — addressing the "end of history illusion" that makes reinvention feel impossible rather than navigational.*

> Act as my transformation partner and mapmaker. Here is where I am right now: [DESCRIBE YOUR CURRENT ROLE, STRENGTHS, KEY HABITS, FEARS, WHAT PEOPLE REWARD YOU FOR, AND ANY OTHER RELEVANT CONTEXT]. Here is where I am trying to go: [DESCRIBE YOUR NEXT CHAPTER — new job, career pivot, relationship, health reset, life phase, etc.]. Interview me one question at a time to fully understand the gap. Then give me a practical transformation map that answers these five questions: (1) Which of my current strengths will carry over? (2) Which strengths will become liabilities in this new chapter? (3) What do I need to leave behind, even if it has worked before? (4) What have I never built but will now need? (5) What should I test for at least 30 days before making a bigger commitment? Do not give me abstract purpose advice. Do not give me a 10-year life plan. Do not flatter me. Give me a practical, actionable map.

---

#### Prompt 4: Decision Pre-Mortem Simulator
*Runs Gary Klein's pre-mortem technique on both sides of a difficult fork — simulating failure 2 years out and working backward to surface risks you're too excited or too afraid to see clearly.*

> Act as my decision simulator. I am facing a fork and need to pressure-test both options before choosing. Option A is: [DESCRIBE OPTION A]. Option B is: [DESCRIBE OPTION B]. Here is what I know about my situation — constraints, resources, timeline, people involved, and stakes: [ADD ALL RELEVANT CONTEXT]. Interview me one question at a time to fully understand the decision. Then run a separate pre-mortem on each option independently: imagine it is 2 years later and that choice failed badly — work backward and explain why. For each option, tell me: (1) the most likely way it will fail, (2) the early warning signs I should watch for, and (3) the cost I cannot reverse if it goes wrong. Treat the two options independently so I can compare how each road fails differently. Do not tell me which option to choose. Be rigorous and systematic. Do not flatter me.

---

#### Prompt 5: Failure and Setback Debrief Partner
*Structures a 15-minute reflective writing session after failure or loss — based on UT Austin research showing that written processing of painful events produces measurably better long-term health outcomes.*

> Act as a debrief partner for something difficult I have gone through. Here is what happened: [DESCRIBE THE FAILURE, LOSS, SETBACK, REJECTION, OR BETRAYAL IN PLAIN LANGUAGE]. The story I have been telling myself about this is: [SHARE YOUR CURRENT NARRATIVE — about yourself, about others, about trust, risk, or the future]. Interview me one question at a time. Do not comfort me or rush toward a solution — I want to talk and think clearly. When you have enough from me, help me understand four things: (1) the objective facts of what happened, (2) the meaning I keep adding that isn't supported by the facts, (3) what was genuinely mine to control and what was not, and (4) one thing worth carrying forward and one thing worth leaving behind. Be honest and direct. Do not perform empathy. Do not flatter me.

---

#### Prompt 6: Hard Feedback Log Analyser
*Builds a structured personal "blind spot profile" by analysing recurring themes across all the critical feedback you've collected over time — turning isolated criticism into actionable self-knowledge.*

> I am going to give you a list of the most significant pieces of critical feedback I have received over my life — from managers, colleagues, partners, mentors, or anyone who knows me well. Here is the list: [PASTE YOUR FEEDBACK LOG — even rough notes or paraphrased memories are fine]. Analyse these for recurring patterns. Identify: (1) the top 2-3 consistent themes across multiple sources, (2) any feedback that appears contradictory and what that might mean, (3) blind spots I seem consistently unaware of, and (4) the single highest-leverage area where change would have the most positive ripple effect on my life and work. Do not soften the analysis. Do not frame this as a therapy session. Give me a clear, evidence-based summary I can act on.
