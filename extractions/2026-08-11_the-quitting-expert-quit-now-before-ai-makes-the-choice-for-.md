### Summary
Seth Godin delivers a wide-ranging masterclass on getting unstuck, strategic quitting, and purposeful living. The core framework centres on identifying hidden "buts" (conflicting desires), recognising the difference between problems (solvable) and situations (to be accepted), and understanding The Dip — the hard part between starting and mastery. Godin argues that strategic quitting, clarity of purpose, and serving a specific audience are superpowers in an AI-accelerated world that increasingly commoditises rule-following work.

---

### Key Insights

- **The "but" trap is why you're stuck.** Every instance of feeling paralysed comes from wanting two incompatible things simultaneously — "I want a creative life *but* I never want to be rejected." Name the "but" out loud; it dissolves the paralysis and forces an honest choice.

- **Problems vs. situations.** A problem has a solution; a situation must be accepted. Misclassifying a situation as a problem wastes your life fighting gravity. Clarity on which you're facing is the first move.

- **The Dip is not a reason to quit — it's a signpost you're on the right path.** Every valuable skill has a dip (organic chemistry for doctors, the gym in February). See it before you start, pack for it, and say "thank you" when it arrives.

- **Strategic quitting is a superpower.** You cannot start something new without quitting something old. The Michael Jordan "never quit" ethos is wrong for most people — it's fuel specific to his psychology and context, not a universal law.

- **Sunk costs are gifts from your former self — you don't have to accept them.** The law degree, the bad lease, the unfulfilling career: these were right decisions *then*. Knowing what you know *now*, you get to decide again.

- **"Who is this for?" is the most clarifying question.** Trying to serve everyone (or the wrong crowd) is the core of being stuck. The Italian tour group didn't speak English — the comedian didn't bomb, the agent put them in the wrong room. Pick the smallest viable audience.

- **Consistency beats authenticity.** Professionals make a promise and keep it. "Authenticity" is often resistance in disguise — a socially acceptable reason to hide and never ship.

- **In an AI world, be unwriteable.** If your entire job can be written down as rules, AI will do it faster and cheaper. The moat is human judgment, taste, trust networks, and connecting people — things that cannot be fully codified.

- **Juggling is about throwing, not catching.** Focus on the practice (the throw), not the outcome (the catch). Get good at the process and results follow. This is why process-focused people consistently outperform outcome-obsessed ones.

- **You decide why, you don't discover it.** Purpose isn't innate — it's chosen. "The day you decide why" is more accurate than "the day you find out why."

### Actions

- [ ] Write down one major area where you feel stuck and identify the hidden "but" — the two things you want simultaneously that cannot coexist.
- [ ] Classify each stuck area as a **problem** (has a solution) or a **situation** (must be accepted) and commit to the appropriate response.
- [ ] Before starting any new project, map the Dip: what is the hardest part, has anyone made it through, and do you have the resources to get there?
- [ ] Identify one thing you should strategically quit — a job, project, or commitment that is a cul-de-sac, not a dip.
- [ ] Write a one-sentence purpose statement for your current main project: "The purpose of this work is \_\_\_ for \_\_\_."
- [ ] Name your smallest viable audience — the 5–25 people who would genuinely miss your work if it disappeared tomorrow.
- [ ] List the sunk costs keeping you in place and reframe each as "a gift from your former self — do I want to accept it today?"
- [ ] Identify the three voices you're currently serving (e.g., parents, Instagram, playground trauma) and consciously choose which one to orient toward.
- [ ] Start the "anonymous blog" equivalent: begin your creative project under low-stakes conditions for 100 days before worrying about identity or audience.
- [ ] Audit your job/role: can someone write down everything you do as rules? If yes, start developing skills in human judgment, taste, or network-building that cannot be codified.
- [ ] For any decision you're avoiding, ask: "Knowing what I know now, would my future self thank me for doing this today?"

### Implementation Prompts

#### Prompt 1: Personal "But" Audit
*Surfaces the hidden conflicting desires that are causing paralysis in your life or work, so you can name them and make a real choice.*

> I'm going to describe areas of my life where I feel stuck. For each one, help me identify the hidden "but" — the two things I want simultaneously that cannot coexist. Then help me rewrite each as an honest "and" statement that forces a clear choice.
>
> Here are my stuck areas: [list 3–5 areas, e.g., "I want to build a startup but I also want to be present for my family", "I want to go freelance but I need financial stability"].
>
> For each one:
> 1. Name the hidden "but" explicitly
> 2. Show what each option actually costs
> 3. Rewrite as an "and" statement (e.g., "I want X AND that means accepting Y")
> 4. Suggest the smallest next decision that would move me forward
>
> Be direct and don't soften the trade-offs. The goal is clarity, not comfort.

---

#### Prompt 2: Problem vs. Situation Classifier
*Stops wasted energy fighting things that cannot be changed, and focuses effort on what actually has a solution.*

> Help me classify the following challenges in my life as either PROBLEMS (which have solutions and are worth fighting) or SITUATIONS (which must be accepted and worked around, like gravity).
>
> For each item I give you, use this framework:
> - Is there a realistic path where this gets better through my action?
> - Has anyone in a similar position solved this before?
> - Is the cost of solving it proportionate to the benefit?
>
> Then give me: (a) the classification, (b) the reasoning, (c) if it's a problem — one concrete first step, (d) if it's a situation — how to accept and work around it productively.
>
> My challenges: [list your challenges here]

---

#### Prompt 3: The Dip Mapper
*Helps you decide whether to push through or quit intelligently, by mapping the hard part of any goal before you're in it.*

> I want to map "The Dip" for a goal I'm pursuing: [describe your goal].
>
> Help me analyse:
> 1. **What is the Dip?** — What is the hardest, most discouraging phase between starting and mastery for this specific goal?
> 2. **Dip or cul-de-sac?** — Is this a temporary hard phase that leads somewhere, or a dead end with no evidence of breakthrough?
> 3. **Has anyone made it through?** — Give me real examples of people who navigated this specific dip successfully.
> 4. **Resource check** — What would I realistically need (time, money, skills, support) to get through the dip? Do I have it?
> 5. **Decision** — Based on this analysis, should I commit fully, quit now, or change my approach?
>
> Be honest. I'd rather quit now than waste years on a cul-de-sac.

---

#### Prompt 4: Smallest Viable Audience Builder
*Replaces vague "build for everyone" thinking with a concrete, specific audience you can actually serve and get traction with.*

> I'm working on [describe your project, product, or creative work]. Help me identify and define my Smallest Viable Audience — the minimum number of specific people I need to serve deeply before anything else matters.
>
> Walk me through:
> 1. **Who exactly** — Describe 3 specific "persona" profiles of people who would genuinely benefit from this work. Be specific: what do they believe, what are they afraid of, what problem do they have?
> 2. **The smallest number** — How many of these people, if I genuinely helped them, would be "enough" to prove the idea works?
> 3. **Where to find them** — Where do these specific people currently gather, complain, or look for help?
> 4. **First contact** — What is the single lowest-barrier action I could take this week to get in front of 5 of these people?
> 5. **Success signal** — How will I know if they actually got value (not just that they were polite)?

---

#### Prompt 5: AI-Proof Career Audit
*Identifies which parts of your current work are vulnerable to AI automation and helps you shift toward uncodifiable, human-centred skills.*

> Audit my current role/work for AI vulnerability using Seth Godin's framework: "If I can write down everything you do as rules, AI can do it faster and cheaper."
>
> My current role/work: [describe your job or main work in detail]
>
> For each major responsibility I have:
> 1. **Codifiability score (1–10)** — How easily could this be written as a ruleset for an AI to follow?
> 2. **Timeline** — How soon is this realistically at risk (1 year / 3 years / 5+ years)?
> 3. **Human moat** — What human judgment, taste, relationships, or context-reading makes this hard to automate?
>
> Then give me:
> - The 2–3 skills I should develop NOW that will compound in an AI-heavy world (think: taste, trust networks, human connection, strategic judgment)
> - One concrete project I could start in the next 30 days that exercises those skills
> - How to reframe my existing role to lead with uncodifiable strengths

---

#### Prompt 6: Sunk Cost Liberation Exercise
*Reframes past investments (careers, degrees, relationships, businesses) from traps into neutral "gifts" you can choose to accept or decline.*

> I'm going to list things I feel "trapped" by due to sunk costs. For each one, help me run the Seth Godin "gift from your former self" reframe.
>
> My sunk costs: [list them — e.g., "10 years in a career I don't enjoy", "a business I've built but that isn't working", "a degree I'm not using"]
>
> For each one:
> 1. **Acknowledge the gift** — What did your former self give you by making this investment? (Skills, identity, experience, options)
> 2. **The honest question** — "Knowing what you know NOW, would you choose to accept this gift going forward?"
> 3. **The real cost of staying** — What does continuing to honour this sunk cost actually cost you per year (time, energy, opportunity, fulfilment)?
> 4. **The quit scenario** — If you walked away from this tomorrow, what specifically would you lose vs. gain in 1 year / 5 years?
> 5. **The forgiveness statement** — Draft a one-paragraph statement forgiving your former self for creating this situation, releasing you to decide fresh.

---

#### Prompt 7: Purpose Statement Workshop
*Builds a clear, testable one-sentence purpose for your current work that guides decisions and cuts through distraction.*

> Help me write a precise purpose statement for my current main project or work: [describe the project/work].
>
> The format should be: "The purpose of [this work] is to [specific change] for [specific who]."
>
> Walk me through it:
> 1. **The who** — Push me to get more specific. Not "people who want to get healthier" but something like "35–45 year old professionals who've tried diets and failed and are embarrassed by it."
> 2. **The change** — What specifically changes for them after engaging with my work? What do they believe, feel, or do differently?
> 3. **The test** — How would I know in 90 days if I'm actually achieving this purpose?
> 4. **The wrong voices** — Who am I currently NOT serving with this work? (Naming who it's not for is as important as naming who it is for.)
> 5. **Draft three versions** of the purpose statement, ranging from broad to razor-specific, and explain the trade-offs of each.
