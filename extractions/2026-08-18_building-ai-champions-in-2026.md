### Summary
Manu Life's AI enablement lead shares a detailed, practitioner-level breakdown of how they built and scaled an AI Champions Network across a global insurance organisation. The conversation covers everything from securing executive sponsorship and selecting the right champions, to onboarding design, goal-setting, measurement, and sustaining momentum. It's a pragmatic, experience-backed playbook for anyone looking to drive bottom-up AI adoption through a structured human network.

---

### Key Insights

- **Start with organisational readiness, not the network itself.** Before launching, ensure there's baseline AI awareness, learning programs, and executive sponsorship — otherwise champions have nothing to amplify.
- **Mirror your organisation's culture in how you launch.** Ask "how do things get done here?" — top-down cultures suit a mandated rollout; bottom-up cultures suit organic champion identification and grassroots momentum.
- **Keep the core champion group small and selective.** Manu Life deliberately chose a small, globally distributed core group based on influence, performance data, and leadership endorsement — quality over coverage.
- **Two goals for MVP1 is enough.** They focused solely on (1) raising AI literacy and (2) driving adoption of two specific productivity tools — measurable, achievable, and contextual to each team.
- **Empower, don't dictate.** Champions were given outcome targets and a resource inventory (promptathons, demos, office hours, dashboards), then asked to build their own road map for their area.
- **Less is more in onboarding.** A 30-60-90 day onboarding journey that chunks information over time prevents overwhelming high-performers who already carry heavy workloads.
- **Bi-weekly touchpoints sustain momentum.** A monthly champion forum (updates + peer sharing) and optional bi-weekly office hours, combined with a live Teams channel, keeps the network connected and cross-pollinating ideas.
- **Equip champions for the human side of AI.** Partner with change management and communications teams to train champions on empathy, overcoming resistance, and delivering a responsible AI narrative — especially around job security concerns.
- **Measurement enables momentum.** Tracking adoption rates, learning completions, and survey-based literacy confidence scores not only proves value but sustains champion and leadership engagement over time.

### Actions

- [ ] Audit your organisation's "readiness" before designing the program: Is there executive awareness of AI? Existing learning pathways? Tool access?
- [ ] Identify 3–5 senior leaders who could act as visible sponsors and schedule conversations to socialise the concept and get explicit endorsement.
- [ ] Map 2–3 "pockets of readiness" in your organisation — teams with curious, high-performing individuals and supportive managers — as starting points for champion placement.
- [ ] Define selection criteria for lead champions: influence/respect, performance data, and leader endorsement (not just AI enthusiasm).
- [ ] Draft exactly two measurable goals for MVP1 — e.g., "Raise AI literacy (track via learning completions + survey)" and "Increase tool adoption by X% in each champion's area."
- [ ] Build a 30-60-90 day onboarding journey: Day 1–30 = role clarity + resources; Day 31–60 = learning pathway completion; Day 61–90 = data review + road map creation.
- [ ] Create a resource inventory (promptathon kit, demo slide deck, walking/talking AI deck, FAQ) that champions can pick from — not mandate — to hit their goals.
- [ ] Schedule a recurring bi-weekly cadence: one monthly champion forum (updates + peer sharing) and one optional office hours session.
- [ ] Set up a dedicated real-time channel (Teams/Slack) for the champion network for async updates and peer questions.
- [ ] Partner with your change management or HR function to train champions on empathy, resistance handling, and responsible AI messaging.
- [ ] Establish a reporting cadence back to senior leadership — use existing meeting structures where possible — to tell the story of wins and sustain buy-in.
- [ ] If top-down sponsorship isn't available, identify natural "unofficial champions," create a badge/recognition mechanism, and start a monthly informal call to build grassroots momentum.

### Implementation Prompts

#### Prompt 1: Champion Selection Scorecard
*Helps you operationalise the three-lens selection criteria (influence, performance, leadership endorsement) into a repeatable tool you can use with managers.*

> Create a Champion Selection Scorecard in Markdown table format for identifying AI Champions within a large enterprise. Use three evaluation lenses based on this framework:
> 1. **Influence** – Is this person respected and trusted by peers? Do colleagues seek them out for advice? (Rate 1–5)
> 2. **Performance** – Are they a recognised high performer based on available data or manager assessment? (Rate 1–5)
> 3. **Leadership Endorsement** – Has their direct manager explicitly endorsed them for this role? (Yes/No + notes)
>
> Include a fourth column for "AI Engagement History" (Have they participated in any AI learning, experimentation, or tool use in the past 12 months? Yes/No + examples).
>
> Add a scoring guide at the bottom: what score threshold qualifies someone as a Lead Champion vs. a Network Champion (informal). Also include a short paragraph of guidance on how to present this scorecard to senior leaders to get their nominations.

#### Prompt 2: MVP1 Goal Definition Framework
*Helps teams define exactly two measurable, achievable goals for their champions program's first year — avoiding scope creep and vague objectives.*

> Help me define two clear, measurable goals for an AI Champions Program MVP1 (first year). The goals should follow this structure:
>
> **Goal 1: AI Literacy**
> - What does "literacy" mean in our context? (e.g., understanding what AI is, knowing what tools are available, basic prompting ability)
> - How will we measure it? (e.g., % of team completing foundational AI learning pathway, pre/post survey confidence scores)
> - What is the target? (e.g., 80% of teams in champion's area complete foundational pathway within 6 months)
>
> **Goal 2: Tool Adoption**
> - Which specific tool(s) are we driving adoption of?
> - What is the current baseline adoption rate?
> - What is the target increase (e.g., +20% active users in champion's area within 6 months)?
> - How will we track it? (e.g., tool usage dashboard, weekly active users)
>
> Output a one-page goal brief in clean Markdown that a champion could print and reference. Include a section for the champion to fill in their own area-specific targets. Also add a short "what success looks like at 30, 60, and 90 days" timeline for each goal.

#### Prompt 3: 30-60-90 Day Champion Onboarding Plan
*Generates a structured onboarding journey that prevents cognitive overload and helps champions integrate their new role alongside their day job.*

> Design a 30-60-90 day onboarding plan for newly selected AI Champions in a large enterprise organisation. The champion role is a "side of desk" responsibility added to their existing full-time job. Structure the plan as follows:
>
> **Days 1–30: Orient**
> - Role clarity (what they are and are not responsible for)
> - Introduction to their two measurable goals
> - Access to the resource inventory (list of available tools, decks, programs)
> - Completion of a foundational AI learning pathway (online, self-paced, ~2–3 hours total)
> - First attendance at the Champion Forum
>
> **Days 31–60: Learn**
> - Deep dive into measurement: how to read adoption dashboards, how to track literacy metrics
> - Review of available activation resources (promptathon kit, demo template, walking deck)
> - Draft their own area road map and objectives (with support from office hours if needed)
> - Share draft road map with their manager for alignment
>
> **Days 61–90: Activate**
> - Run first activation event (e.g., promptathon, lunch-and-learn, or demo) in their area
> - Review early data and iterate
> - Share learnings at monthly champion forum
>
> Format as a clean Markdown checklist with estimated time commitment per week (should not exceed 2–3 hours/week). Add a "support available" note next to each milestone showing where champions can get help.

> - Lunch-and-learn slide deck template

> - Office hours facilitation guide
>
> **3. Measurement & Reporting**
> - Tool adoption dashboard (how to access + interpret)
> - Literacy survey template (pre/post)
> - Learning completion report guide
> - Champion road map template
>
> **4. Support Channels**
> - Monthly champion forum (schedule + how to join)
> - Bi-weekly office hours (link + agenda format)

#### Prompt 6: Champion Forum Agenda Template
*Generates a repeatable, energising bi-weekly forum structure that keeps champions connected, informed, and cross-pollinating ideas.*

> Create a repeatable monthly Champion Forum agenda template for an AI Champions Network. The forum is 60 minutes, held virtually, and attended by 15–30 champions globally across different functions and time zones. It should balance updates from the central AI team with peer-to-peer sharing.
>
> Structure:
>
> **0:00–0:05** — Welcome + housekeeping (what's new, any urgent updates)
> **0:05–0:15** — "What's changed this month" update from AI enablement team (new tools, new learning programs, policy changes, answered FAQs)
> **0:15–0:35** — Champion spotlight (1–2 champions share what they've been doing in their area — what worked, what didn't, lessons learned). Include a template for the presenting champion to fill in before the call.
> **0:35–0:50** — Open discussion / Q&A (champions ask each other questions, share challenges, brainstorm solutions)
> **0:50–0:55** — Upcoming dates and actions (next office hours, any deadlines, new resources dropping)
> **0:55–1:00** — Closing energiser or shoutout (recognise a champion win from the past month)
>
> Also include:
> - A pre-meeting preparation checklist for the facilitator
> - A post-meeting follow-up template (what to send to champions within 24 hours of the call)
> - A "Champion Spotlight" submission form template (so champions know how to prepare their 10-minute share)
>
> Format in clean Markdown suitable for a Teams wiki or Notion page.

#### Prompt 7: AI Literacy Measurement Framework
*Translates the vague goal of "raising AI literacy" into concrete, trackable leading and lagging indicators with survey templates.*

> Build an AI Literacy Measurement Framework for an enterprise AI Champions Program. The framework needs to cover both leading indicators (actions that predict literacy growth) and lagging indicators (evidence that literacy has actually improved).
>
> Structure:
>
> **Leading Indicators**
> - % of employees in champion's area who have started a foundational AI learning pathway
> - % who have completed it
> - Number of activation events run (promptathons, demos, lunch-and-learns) per quarter
> - Number of attendees across those events
>
> **Lagging Indicators**
> - Pre/post confidence survey (design a 5-question survey with a 1–5 Likert scale covering: understanding of what AI is, awareness of available tools, ability to write a basic prompt, comfort using AI in daily work, understanding of responsible AI use)
> - Tool adoption rate change (% active users before vs. after champion-led activation)
> - Qualitative stories / anecdotes collected from teams
>
> **Measurement Cadence**
> - What to measure monthly vs. quarterly vs. annually
> - Who owns each metric (champion vs. central AI team vs. HR/L&D)
>
> Output the full framework as a Markdown document including the 5-question literacy confidence survey (ready to copy into Microsoft Forms or Google Forms), a simple dashboard layout description, and a one-paragraph "how to tell the story" guide for reporting results to senior leaders.
