### Summary
James Sexton, a New York divorce lawyer with 25 years of experience, shares hard-won insights on marriage, prenups, and relationships. His core argument is that everyone already has a prenup — either written by two people in love or imposed by the government — and that the frank, vulnerable conversations required to negotiate one are themselves predictive of relationship success. Drawing on thousands of real cases, he offers unusually practical wisdom on how to argue well, avoid weaponizing intimacy, and know when to leave.

---

### Key Insights

- **Everyone has a prenup.** Either you write it with your partner while you still love each other, or the state writes it for you — and the state can change it at any time without your consent. Framing it this way removes the stigma entirely.

- **Prenups may actually reduce divorce rates.** In 25 years, Sexton has done hundreds of prenups but only three divorces for prenup clients. The open, vulnerable conversation required to negotiate one is itself a relationship skill that predicts longevity.

- **Introduce the prenup topic on the third date** — casually, by referencing a celebrity couple. Gauge your partner's temperature before making it personal. This is early enough to be natural and informative.

- **Never weaponize intimacy.** The most destructive thing in a relationship is using your partner's disclosed vulnerabilities against them in a fight. It cannot be unsaid and relationships rarely recover from it.

- **The lows, not the highs, determine relationship success.** Reality TV and romantic experiences are poor predictors of compatibility. How a couple handles conflict, confusion, and stress is what actually goes the distance.

- **Marriage doesn't change people — and it doesn't preserve them either.** Believing a relationship will fix someone's bad habits, OR that marriage will lock in the current good state, are both equally dangerous illusions.

- **Framing matters enormously in difficult conversations.** "Why aren't we having sex as much?" versus "I was driving past the restaurant where we first dated..." produce completely different emotional responses and outcomes.

- **Post-divorce recovery doesn't begin until the divorce is legally final.** Processing grief, maintaining a body practice, re-establishing routines, and having something to love (children, pet, community) are the key recovery tools.

- **Women are the gatekeepers of sexual standards, and men will meet whatever standard is set.** Baumeister's research suggests that the standards women hold collectively shape male behaviour — including whether courtship and commitment are required.
