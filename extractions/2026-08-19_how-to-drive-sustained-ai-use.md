# How to Drive Sustained AI Use

### Summary
This session by Lee Purvis (Section) focuses on the human infrastructure required to sustain AI adoption after the initial launch — the often-ignored "people layer" that determines whether AI transformation succeeds or flatlines. The core argument is that 70% of AI value comes from people, not technology, and the real gap is mastery and reinforcement, not awareness. Four requirements drive sustained adoption: clarity, purpose, fluency, and belonging — with practical frameworks for leaders, champions, peer learning, and measurement.

---

### Key Insights

- **Usage spikes are not adoption.** Organizations consistently see a launch spike followed by a 3-month flatline — not because of resistance, but because demand was built without infrastructure.
- **70% of AI value comes from people, not technology.** BCG research across hundreds of companies confirms most organizations are underinvesting in the highest-value lever.
- **Managers who *expect* (not just encourage) AI use produce employees 2.6x more proficient.** Expectation is structural; encouragement is optional. Companies where 88% of managers role-model AI vs. 25% at stalling companies is one of the most predictive data points available.
- **85% of workers use AI only as a search/summarization tool** — not because they're lazy, but because they haven't been shown anything else. This is an imagination gap, not a skills gap.
- **Hyper-personalized coaching beats generic curriculum.** The goal is helping each person find 2–3 specific tasks where AI genuinely multiplies their output. Generic training doesn't do this.
- **Use case libraries turn individual wins into organizational infrastructure.** Companies with prompt/use case libraries see 3–4x higher adoption among non-technical employees.
- **Champions need structure, not just enthusiasm.** A champion without dedicated time, a clear role, and organizational backing is just a volunteer — and volunteers burn out. Atlassian's model (10–20% time allocation, manager buy-in, self-set goals) is the benchmark.
- **The 50/50 framework sets healthy expectations.** Telling employees upfront that ~50% of AI experiments will deliver real value prevents failed experiments from being interpreted as evidence that AI "doesn't work for them."
- **Demo days, not hackathons, drive adoption spikes.** Dapr went from 10% to 50%+ weekly AI usage in one week — driven by peer-to-peer learning at the demo day, not the hackathon itself.
- **Scale requires real investment.** For a 20,000-person org: 17,000 people-hours/year, 300 champion meetings, 1,200 lunch-and-learns, 100 hackathons. A 2-person AI team with good intentions cannot do this.

### Actions

- [ ] Audit your current manager cohort: are they *expecting* AI use or just *encouraging* it? Identify the gap between the two behaviours.
- [ ] Set up private executive coaching sessions where senior leaders work through their own AI use cases without an audience.
- [ ] Implement a quarterly demo requirement for every leader — one use case, no polish required, normalized imperfection.
- [ ] Run a pulse survey + review LLM usage logs + collect manager nominations to identify your first cohort of champions (aim for 5% of workforce).
- [ ] Define champion roles formally: document responsibilities, allocate 10–20% of their time, let them set their own goals with manager sign-off.
- [ ] Launch a use case library with a standard template: use case description, time saved, step-by-step instructions, and a contact for questions.
- [ ] Schedule recurring peer learning formats: monthly lunch-and-learns, quarterly hackathons with mandatory demo days, and a Slack/Teams channel for AI shout-outs.
- [ ] Communicate the 50/50 framework to all employees before any sharing events begin — normalize failed experiments explicitly.
- [ ] Make managers (not the central AI team) responsible for tracking and recording team-level value gains on a quarterly basis.
- [ ] Establish your four core metrics and baseline them now: weekly active users, L3 proficiency %, L4 expert %, agent adoption ratio per employee.
- [ ] Have an honest conversation with leadership about the actual resource investment required (budget, headcount, software) vs. the current allocation.

### Implementation Prompts

#### Prompt 1: Manager Expectation Audit & Activation Plan
*Identifies the gap between "encouraging" and "expecting" AI use among managers and produces a concrete activation plan with talking points and structural changes.*

> I'm building an AI adoption program for a large organisation. One of our biggest gaps is that managers are encouraging AI use verbally but not structurally embedding it into team operations. Based on research showing employees whose managers actively *expect* AI usage are 2.6x more proficient, I need to close this gap.
>
> Please produce:
> 1. A self-assessment rubric (5-point scale) that managers can use to honestly rate their current AI role-modelling behaviour across 6 dimensions (e.g., mentions AI in 1:1s, demos AI in meetings, etc.)
> 2. A concrete list of 10 structural changes managers can make to shift from "encouraging" to "expecting" AI use within their teams — including changes to meeting agendas, 1:1 templates, and performance conversations
> 3. A short manager briefing email template (150 words max) that explains the shift from encouragement to expectation, why it matters, and what they're being asked to do differently starting this week
> 4. Three example "AI experiment" discussion questions managers can add as standing agenda items to team meetings
>
> Tone: direct, practical, non-jargon. These managers are busy and skeptical of yet another initiative.

#### Prompt 2: Champion Identification & Scoring Framework
*Creates a data-driven process for identifying, scoring, and recruiting your first champion cohort from across the organisation.*

> I need to identify AI champions across a large organisation (5,000–25,000 employees). Champions should represent ~5% of the workforce, embedded across all functions — not just tech. The best candidates combine: self-identified interest in AI, actual power-user behaviour (visible in usage data), and manager endorsement.
>
> Please produce:
> 1. A pulse survey (8–10 questions max) to identify employees already experimenting with AI — include questions that surface credibility, functional expertise, and communication ability, not just enthusiasm
> 2. A scoring rubric that weights three data sources: pulse survey responses, LLM usage data signals (e.g., session frequency, prompt complexity, breadth of tools used), and manager nomination strength
> 3. A champion role description document including: mission statement, key responsibilities (3 jobs: tactical coaching, peer support infrastructure, workflow transformation), time commitment (10–20%), and what success looks like in 90 days
> 4. A one-page "why become a champion" pitch that explains what's in it for them — recognition, skills, visibility, and impact — without overselling or making it sound like unpaid extra work
>
> Format each as a standalone document I can adapt and deploy immediately.

#### Prompt 3: Use Case Library — Template & Submission System Design
*Designs a practical, low-friction use case library that converts individual AI wins into shared organisational assets.*

> I want to build an internal AI use case library that turns individual employee discoveries into organisational infrastructure. Research shows companies with prompt/use case libraries see 3–4x higher AI adoption among non-technical employees.
>
> Please design the complete system:
> 1. A standardised use case submission template with these required fields: use case title, function/team, task description, time saved per week (estimate), step-by-step instructions to replicate, tools/models used, contact person, skill level required (beginner/intermediate/advanced), and a "lessons learned / what didn't work" section
> 2. A tagging/categorisation taxonomy for organising entries by: function (Marketing, Finance, Engineering, etc.), skill level, business goal (speed, quality, cost, creativity), and use case type (writing, analysis, research, automation, etc.)
> 3. A lightweight governance process: how submissions get reviewed, who approves them (suggest champion-led), and how often the library gets audited for quality
> 4. Three example fully-completed use case entries across different functions (e.g., Sales, HR, Finance) so employees understand the standard of detail expected
> 5. A "before you start from scratch" nudge — a short prompt or checklist employees see before starting a new AI workflow, reminding them to check the library first
>
> Prioritise simplicity and low friction. The library dies if submission is too burdensome.

#### Prompt 4: Hyper-Personalised AI Coaching Conversation Framework
*Builds a structured coaching conversation guide that helps any manager or champion close the "imagination gap" with individual employees.*

> Most employees (85%) use AI only as a search or summarisation tool — not because they lack motivation, but because they've never been shown concretely how AI applies to their specific work. I need a coaching conversation framework that helps managers and champions run a 30–45 minute 1:1 with any employee to identify their personal high-value AI use cases.
>
> Please produce:
> 1. A structured conversation guide with 4 phases: (a) understanding their current role and top 5 time-consuming tasks, (b) identifying tasks that are repetitive, output-heavy, or require synthesis, (c) mapping those tasks to specific AI capabilities with concrete examples, (d) committing to one experiment in the next 2 weeks
> 2. A "task heat map" worksheet the employee fills out before the conversation — rating each of their regular tasks on two axes: time consumed vs. AI suitability
> 3. A reference card for the coach with 20 role-agnostic "imagination gap" prompts — questions designed to help someone picture AI in their actual workflow (e.g., "What's a task you do weekly that always takes longer than it should?")
> 4. A follow-up email template the champion/manager sends after the session summarising the agreed experiment, the expected outcome, and the check-in date
> 5. A 90-day progression path: what "beginner → intermediate → advanced" AI use looks like for a generic knowledge worker, with milestone markers
>
> Tone: coaching, not training. This is about helping someone discover, not instructing them.

#### Prompt 5: Peer Learning Event Playbook (Hackathon + Demo Day Design)
*Produces a complete, reusable event playbook for running hackathons and demo days that generate peer-to-peer adoption spikes.*

> Research shows that demo days — not the hackathons themselves — are what drive AI adoption spikes. Dapr went from 10% to 50%+ weekly AI usage after a company-wide hackathon with a mandatory demo day. I need a complete event playbook I can hand to an organiser and run repeatedly.
>
> Please produce a full playbook covering:
> 1. **Half-day Hackathon Format**: agenda template (with times), facilitator guide, team structure recommendations, challenge brief template (how to frame the AI build challenge), and the "rules" that keep it productive without being prescriptive
> 2. **Demo Day Design**: how to structure the showcase (max 3 mins per team), judging criteria (emphasise learning and process over polish), audience engagement mechanisms, and how to capture outputs for the use case library afterwards
> 3. **Pre-event communications**: invitation email, manager briefing, and a "what to expect" FAQ that addresses "what if mine doesn't work?" anxieties
> 4. **Monthly Lunch-and-Learn Template**: 30-minute agenda, facilitation guide, how to recruit presenters (including people sharing failures), and a simple feedback form to improve each session
> 5. **Slack/Teams "AI Wins" Channel Launch Kit**: channel description, launch message from a leader, posting guidelines that normalise both wins and failures, and how champions moderate it to keep energy high
>
> Flag the one design principle that must not be compromised: the moment where peers watch peers experiment live.

#### Prompt 6: AI Adoption Metrics Dashboard Design
*Builds a practical measurement framework aligned to the four core metrics, accounting for the real challenges of baselining and lagging business impact.*

> I need to design a practical AI adoption measurement framework for a large organisation. The four core metrics I'm tracking are: (1) weekly active users %, (2) L3 proficiency % (confident, skilled, integrated into real work), (3) L4 expert % (workflow transformation level), (4) agent adoption ratio (agents per employee). Measurement in AI transformation is genuinely hard — no baselines, no standard cross-function metric, and business impact lags behaviour change.
>
> Please produce:
> 1. A definition document for each of the four metrics: what exactly counts, how to measure it (data sources), and what "good" looks like at 3, 6, and 12 months (benchmark targets: 80% WAU, 80% L3, 20% L4, 5% champions, 3 agents/employee)
> 2. A quarterly manager reporting template (one page max) that captures: team-level AI usage, notable use cases identified, estimated time saved, blockers, and one thing they need from the AI team
> 3. A proficiency rubric for L1–L4: describe what each level looks like behaviourally in plain language so managers can self-assess their teams without needing technical knowledge
> 4. A "leading indicator" dashboard for the first 90 days (before you have business impact data): what early signals predict long-term adoption success
> 5. A short executive summary template (half-page) for monthly leadership reporting that connects adoption metrics to business outcomes — even when the data is still early and imperfect
>
> Prioritise honesty and simplicity over impressive-looking dashboards. The goal is useful signal, not vanity metrics.

#### Prompt 7: Leadership Activation Programme — 90-Day Plan
*Designs a structured 90-day sequence to activate executives and senior managers as visible, credible AI role models.*

> I need to design a 90-day leadership activation programme for AI adoption. The goal: move leaders from passively supportive to actively role-modelling AI use in the open. Key levers: private executive coaching on their own use cases, monthly AI talks led by champions (not IT), quarterly demo requirements, and helping leaders prepare for employee questions about job security and appropriate AI use.
>
> Please produce:
> 1. A 90-day programme calendar showing: week-by-week sequencing of private coaching sessions, champion-led talks, demo requirements, and communication moments
> 2. A private executive coaching session agenda (60 minutes): how to run a session that helps a senior leader identify and build their own AI use case without embarrassment or performance pressure
> 3. A "leader FAQ" document covering the 5 most common employee questions leaders must be prepared to answer: What if I use AI wrong? What does this mean for jobs? Am I allowed to do this? Is my data safe? How do I know if I'm doing it right?
> 4. A quarterly demo brief for leaders: what they're being asked to demo, what counts as acceptable (low bar deliberately), and talking points for framing imperfection as intentional
> 5. A "sharing the mess" coaching guide — how to help leaders talk about their own failed or messy AI experiments in a way that gives employees permission to be beginners, rather than only sharing polished success stories
>
> Critical design principle: leaders sharing failures publicly is more valuable than success stories. The whole programme should reinforce this.
