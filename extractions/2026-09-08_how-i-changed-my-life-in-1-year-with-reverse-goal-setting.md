# How I Changed My Life in 1 Year with Reverse Goal Setting

### Summary
Justin Sung presents "Reverse Goal Setting," a 5-step framework that works backwards from your desired outcome to build a clear, actionable path. Unlike conventional goal setting (which focuses on *what* to achieve), this method prioritises *who you need to become* — your skills, habits, and attributes — to make achieving the goal a natural result of personal growth. Sung used this method to successfully transition from medical doctor to full-time entrepreneur/educator in under a year.

---

### Key Insights

- **Normal goal setting fails because it focuses on outcomes, not processes.** Knowing what you want doesn't tell you how to get there — the uncertainty is what makes hard goals hard.
- **Your real goal is a feeling, not an outcome.** Like Seth Godin's drill analogy: you don't want the drill, the hole, or even the painting — you want the *feeling* you expect the painting to give you. Anchor goals to that underlying "why."
- **Don't set goals more than 3 years out.** Plans that span too far become fragile — one broken link derails everything. Shorter sprints with regular re-evaluation are more effective.
- **The "meta goal" is the person, not the outcome.** Define who would find your goal *easy to achieve*, then become that person. Outcomes are symptoms of the process; control the process.
- **Gap analysis is your actual plan.** Score required skills (e.g. time management, focus, learning ability) at their needed level vs. your current level. The gap *is* the roadmap.
- **Force field analysis reveals hidden leverage.** Mapping barriers and drivers every few months shows what to remove and what to amplify — preventing wasted effort on the wrong priorities.
- **Focus on 1–2 improvements per month maximum.** Trying to fix too many habits simultaneously guarantees improvement in none. Deliberate sequencing is how compounding works.
- **"I don't know" is a valid and useful answer.** If you can't specify requirements or rate your current ability, that ignorance *is* your first action item — go find out.

### Actions

- [ ] Write down your 5–10 year vision (keep it broad) and underneath it, write the *why* — the feeling or situation you expect it to create
- [ ] Break that vision into a medium-term goal (max 3 years, ideally shorter)
- [ ] Define your "meta goal": describe the person for whom achieving this goal would feel easy — list their specific skills, habits, attributes, and opportunities
- [ ] Rate each required skill/attribute out of 10 (required level vs. your current level) across at least these 5 areas: time management, task prioritisation, focus/concentration, procrastination resistance, learning ability
- [ ] Do a force field analysis: draw your current self → meta goal arc; list barriers on one side and existing drivers/resources/network on the other
- [ ] Identify the single biggest gap or blocker and make it your #1 priority this month
- [ ] Schedule a specific daily or weekly practice for that one priority into your calendar
- [ ] Set a reminder to re-do the force field analysis every 2–3 months
- [ ] Give yourself explicit permission (write it down) to pivot your long-term goal if new evidence shows it won't deliver the "why" you identified

#### Prompt 4: Force Field Analysis
*Maps what's working for you and against you, so you invest energy where it has maximum leverage.*

> Help me conduct a Force Field Analysis for my goal using Justin Sung's reverse goal setting framework.
>
> **My medium-term goal (1–3 years):** [INSERT GOAL]
> **My meta goal (the person I need to become):** [BRIEF DESCRIPTION]
> **My current self:** [BRIEF DESCRIPTION OF WHERE YOU ARE NOW]
>
> Please help me identify and organise:
>
> **BARRIERS (forces working against me):**
> - Skill/habit deficits
> - Environmental obstacles
> - Resource or access limitations
> - Psychological blocks (fear, perfectionism, etc.)
> - External circumstances
>
> **DRIVERS (forces working for me):**
> - Existing strengths and skills
> - Current habits that support the goal
> - Network and relationships I can leverage
> - Resources and tools I have access to
> - Environmental advantages
> - Past experiences relevant to this goal
>
> After listing these, please:
> 1. Identify the 2–3 most critical barriers to address first
> 2. Identify the 2–3 most powerful drivers to deliberately amplify
> 3. Suggest one specific action to reduce each critical barrier
> 4. Suggest one specific action to amplify each key driver
>
> Format as a visual table where possible.

#### Prompt 6: Identify Your Real "Why" Behind a Goal
*Prevents the trap of achieving a goal that doesn't actually deliver what you wanted — applies the Seth Godin drill analogy to your specific situation.*

> I want to stress-test whether my stated goal actually connects to what I really want, using the framework from Justin Sung's reverse goal setting (inspired by Seth Godin's drill analogy).
>
> **My stated goal is:** [INSERT GOAL]
>
> Please help me dig down through the layers:
> 1. What do I think achieving this goal will *give me* or *make possible*?
> 2. What feeling or life situation am I actually trying to create? (go at least 3 levels deep — keep asking "but what does THAT give me?")
> 3. Are there other paths to that same feeling/situation that I might not be considering?
> 4. What would it look like if I achieved this goal but it *didn't* deliver that feeling? (stress test)
> 5. Based on this, is my stated goal actually the right goal — or should I reframe it?
>
> Be honest and a little challenging. The point is to make sure I'm not spending years pursuing something that won't give me what I actually want. Output a clear "Goal Clarity Summary" at the end.
